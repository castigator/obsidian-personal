**Purpose:** scaffolding for modelling an alternative to selling the house on the open market —
Heather buys out Rupert's share and keeps Maple Tree House, Rupert takes his share in cash/other
assets. Set up 24 Aug 2026, ready to work through when Rupert picks this up — **not yet modelled
in detail**, this is structure + known inputs, not conclusions.

## The idea, in one paragraph

Instead of both parties waiting for the subsidence/drain claims to resolve and then marketing the
house (see [[House Sale]] — the "timing dilemma"), Heather buys Rupert's ~50% equity share
directly, most likely by remortgaging the house into her sole name. This would need to clear the
existing RBS balance plus raise enough additional borrowing to pay Rupert out. Whether this is
realistic hinges almost entirely on Heather's own income and mortgage affordability, and on
whether a lender will touch the property at all while the subsidence claim is still open.

## The core question: can Heather actually afford this?

Not modelled yet. The shape of the analysis, once picked up:

1. **What would Heather need to borrow?** = new mortgage to clear RBS (£309,796.61+) + cash to pay
   Rupert his share (from the table above, depending on valuation basis and whether the debt is
   settled first).
2. **Standard mortgage-affordability multiples** (~4–4.5x gross income is the rough rule of thumb
   most residential lenders use) against Heather's income (~£1,471/mo net, so gross materially
   higher but still modest) will almost certainly fall far short of what's needed to refinance the
   whole balance plus a buyout payment solo, unless she has other income/assets not currently on
   file, or a partner/guarantor.
3. **Active subsidence claim is likely a hard blocker** regardless of affordability — per
   Michaels, lenders won't lend against the property while the claim is open. That would push any
   buyout to after Feb–Apr 2027 repairs at the earliest, same timing pressure as a sale.
4. **Alternative financing** worth scoping if the numbers don't work on a standard mortgage alone:
   family loan (her parents have already loaned £8,840.66 into the marriage — appetite for more is
   unknown), extending the term/rate to lower monthly cost, or a partial buyout (Heather takes a
   larger equity share now, Rupert retains a smaller charge redeemable later — more complex, would
   need Alan's input on whether it's even a sensible structure for a consent order).
5. **Cash-settlement option from GHG** (confirmed available in lieu of contractor repairs) could
   accelerate point 3 if it clears the "active claim" blocker faster than waiting for the
   contractor's own timeline — worth cross-referencing once this scenario gets picked up.

## Why this might still be worth pursuing despite the affordability headwind

- Avoids ~£4,000/month combined dual-housing carrying cost that a sale-after-repair timeline
  otherwise runs (~13 months to claim resolution ≈ ~£52,000 total, per [[House Sale]]).
- Avoids estate agent fees and the "cash buyers only" discount if forced to sell while the claim
  is still open.
- Keeps Heather in the family home (relevant to any housing-needs argument on her side, and to
  the clean-break case generally).
- Gets Rupert his share out in cash sooner and fully decoupled from the RBS mortgage and the
  subsidence claim's outcome — no ongoing exposure once he's paid out.

## Open questions (need answers before this can be modelled properly)

- Would RBS (or any lender) actually write a new mortgage against this property while the
  subsidence claim is open, or is that a hard no regardless of who's borrowing? Worth a direct
  question to RBS/a broker rather than assuming Michaels' general comment applies identically here.
- Does Heather have any income, assets, or borrowing capacity beyond what's currently on file
  (savings, family support, a partner) that would change the affordability picture?
- Which house valuation should actually anchor a buyout price — market value now (favours
  Heather, lower price) vs post-repair value (favours Rupert, higher price, but requires waiting)?
  This is a negotiating question, not just an arithmetic one.
- If Heather can't raise a full buyout, is a partial/staged buyout worth exploring, or does that
  just recreate the pension-offset-style complexity Alan would need to weigh in on?
- How would this interact with the joint debt (£23,807) and the still-unsigned card split — settled
  as part of the buyout completion, or separately beforehand?
- Has Rupert actually raised this with Heather yet, even informally? (As of 24 Aug 2026: no —
  this is Rupert's own scenario-planning, not a live proposal.)
