## Reasoning / synthesis

- Two independent colleagues (Philip Haines, Jacques Leplat) name a provider with **"Scottish" in
  the name** — most likely **Scottish Equitable**, a real UK pensions insurer active in that era.
  Scott Tunbridge's mention of "Scottish Widows" is a different, similarly-named company and his own
  account is hedged ("can't remember," possibly Netscape not Speedware) — weaker evidence, but
  worth holding in mind in case Scottish Equitable draws a blank.
- **Scottish Equitable rebranded to Aegon UK in 2009** (public record) — this lines up exactly with
  Shash's statement that *his own* pot from the same Speedware-era scheme is now "held with Aegon."
  This is the strongest single corroboration in the thread: two people, two independent memory
  paths (provider name vs current administrator), converging on the same company under its two
  names.
- Mike Murphy's Rothesay mention adds a further hop: **Rothesay Life** is a specialist buy-out
  insurer that has taken on bulk-purchase-annuity transfers from various UK insurers (including
  historically some Aegon/Scottish Equitable back-books) in block deals over the years. If Aegon
  draws a blank or says "transferred out," Rothesay is the next place to ask. Caveat: Mike didn't
  specify *which* pension "was moved to Rothesay" — could be his own, not necessarily the
  Speedware/Infocentre scheme. Treat as a lead, not a confirmed fact.
- Shash's phrasing "I did Rupert under Speedware" is the most direct evidence that **Rupert was
  actively enrolled**, not just eligible — this outweighs the earlier working assumption that
  enrolment status was unknown. Opt-in (not automatic) is confirmed by two people, consistent with
  pre-2012 UK pension law, so absence of a scheme is still possible if Rupert never signed the form
  — but Shash's statement suggests he did.
- Jacques Leplat's detail about **Alan Rowan** (a Speedware/Infocentre manager, presumably) arranging
  a pensions-rep presentation, and paper statements being sent annually "to tell you how much was in
  the pot," is useful colour for framing a Pension Tracing Service enquiry or a direct approach to
  Aegon — it confirms a formal, documented scheme existed with regular member statements, which
  increases the odds Aegon (or a successor) still holds records even 30+ years later.
