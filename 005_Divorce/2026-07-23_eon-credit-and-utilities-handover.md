# Should Rupert care that Heather's taking over Eon while it's sitting in credit?

## The question

Heather told Rupert (WhatsApp, 23 Jul) she's switching the Eon electricity/water account to her
own name/DD for August, and will do oil by DD too once sorted — taking those bills off the shared
80/20 flow. Rupert's instinct: is this actually worth £200/month to him, or is she just going to
run down existing account credit for a few months before her own money is really paying for
anything — credit that's arguably half his, since it built up while the account was funded 80/20?

## What's actually known

- Eon account (A-666D053F, Pebmarsh electricity) was **£528.55 in credit as of 17 Jul 2026**,
  up from £452.84 a month earlier — building at roughly £76/quarter net, i.e. the DD has been
  over-collecting relative to summer usage for a while.
- That credit accrued while the household ran on the 80/20 split (Rupert funding 80% of Pebmarsh
  bills). No information on water or oil account balances — not seen.
- Source is 🔒H (Heather's own mailbox, found via domain access, Rupert not a recipient) — Rupert
  knows this fact but cannot cite the letter itself without revealing he can see her mail.

## The two readings

**Reading 1 — it's real money, and the timing question is legitimate.**
If Heather's new DD amount is set based on estimated future annual usage (which is how energy
suppliers normally calculate it), the existing £528 credit doesn't just vanish — either it sits
there as a buffer that delays her *ever* needing to top up, or Eon issues a refund/reduces the DD
once she takes over billing responsibility and it's recalculated to her name. Either way, for some
months her "I'll pay for it" carries no real cash cost to her — she's spending down a balance that
Rupert's contributions built. If the account took, say, 4–6 months to draw the credit back to
zero at typical usage, that's £200×4–6 = £800–£1,200 of the "saving" Rupert might mentally credit
himself with that isn't actually coming from Heather's income at all.

**Reading 2 — it's small, single-account, and raising it risks more than it's worth.**
- £528.55 is one account, not all three (water and oil balances are unknown — oil in particular is
  usually paid on delivery/top-up, not a rolling credit DD, so there may be no equivalent credit
  there at all).
- Rupert's "half share" of that credit, if you even accept the framing, is ~£264 — smaller than a
  single month's drain excess, and dwarfed by the £14k+ shared card debt already under active
  negotiation.
- **Raising it now stacks a third contentious money topic (cards, RBS gap, now utility-account
  credit) onto a thread where Heather is already "still thinking" about the bigger items.** Given
  Alan's fixed £1,982 fee is conditional on staying amicable, and the pattern so far (17 Jul: "you
  made a bloody contract") shows Heather reacts to anything that reads as Rupert tracking her
  finances too closely, this is a genuine relationship-cost risk for a modestly-sized item.
- It also can't be raised cleanly without revealing how Rupert knows the balance — the figure
  itself is 🔒H. He'd have to either ask an open question ("is Eon in credit? how much?") that
  invites a vague/incomplete answer, or imply knowledge he can't source without prompting "how do
  you know that?"

## Recommendation

**Don't raise the Eon credit figure now.** It's real but small, it's one account out of at least
three, and the 🔒H sourcing problem means Rupert can't cite it precisely without an awkward
follow-up question about how he knows.

**Important distinction (corrected after Rupert pushed back on the original version of this
note):** utility bills are NOT debts or assets, and there's no "consent-order sweep-up" step for
them the way there is for the credit cards or Ann loans. A mortgage or a card balance is a fixed
obligation that exists regardless of use, and has to be formally allocated (hence the written Card
Split Agreement, hence a Recital). Electricity/water/oil are pure running costs, stoppable at
will, never listed anywhere in `Debts.md` or the settlement figures — there's no balance to
allocate, just an ongoing arrangement about who pays going forward. Treating it as something to
"sweep up later" was a category error.

**Nothing further is needed on process.** Heather's 15:06 WhatsApp reply — "I will pay for
electricity / water & oil for Pebmarsh" — already is the confirmation Rupert needs. It's plain
text, volunteered, no resistance (unlike the card-split contract, which she pushed back on
precisely because it's a debt reallocation with legal consequences — a different category of
thing). No document, no formal step, no waiting for consent-order process. Rupert should just stop
putting his 80% into those three Pebmarsh bill lines from August, and optionally send a one-line
acknowledgment back so there's no later ambiguity about when the switch took effect.

- If Rupert ever wants independent confirmation of the Eon credit specifically without relying on
  🔒H knowledge, a neutral household-admin-style question ("worth checking Eon isn't in credit
  before the new DD kicks in — is it?") would surface it legitimately. Not necessary now.
- This stays a minor, quantifiable point (~£264 max, one account, oil/water balances unknown) —
  worth having tracked here for Rupert's own reference, not something to formally chase.

## Addendum (23 Jul, later same day) — the real number is £305.90, not £200

Rupert told Heather (WhatsApp, 15:08) "That's an extra £200/month - you able to cope with that?"
He then queried his own household-budget spreadsheet and asked for this to be checked properly.

**The reconciliation:**
- Three bills being handed over: Anglian Water £82.00 + E.On £107.11 + Certas Energy (oil)
  £193.27 = **£382.38** combined, previously split 80/20 through the joint pot.
- Rupert's prior 80% share: £305.90. Heather's prior 20% share: £76.48.
- Heather now pays the full £382.38 herself. Her **actual** new burden is £382.38 − £76.48 (what
  she already paid) = **£305.90** — not £200.
- This ties out exactly against Rupert's own spreadsheet: his "Scheduled transactions" personal
  line dropped from £575.75 to £239.77 (a £335.98 fall). Of that, £305.90 is the utilities share
  above; the remaining £30.08 is Smarty (£37.60 mobile bill) separately coming out of the shared
  "Share" column in the same spreadsheet edit — a deliberate, already-accepted change, unrelated
  to the utilities handover itself.
- **General principle, not just this instance:** removing any item from the shared 80/20 pot only
  changes Rupert's own payment by 80% of that item's value — never the full value — because he
  was never funding the other 20% himself. Conversely, whoever's now paying the bill in full picks
  up the other party's former share on top of their own, which is exactly why Heather's new burden
  (£305.90) matches Rupert's saving (£305.90) pound for pound.

**Why it matters:** Rupert underquoted Heather by ~£106/month when he described the change to her.
Not corrected with her as of 23 Jul — her 15:12 reply ("It seems only fair as I would have to pay
utilities on my own") accepted the principle without any sign she'd independently checked the
size. Combined with the Eon £528.55 credit buffer discussed above, her real cash-flow exposure
is probably softer and more gradual than either £200 or £305.90 suggests in the short term — but
£305.90 is the correct steady-state figure once that buffer runs out.
