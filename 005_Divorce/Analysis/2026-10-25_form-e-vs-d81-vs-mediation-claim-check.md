# Claim check: "Mediation and a full Form E are the route for people who can't agree; the D81 is the route for people who can" (25 Oct 2026)

**Question:** Rupert asked for deep scrutiny of this line in his draft reply to Heather's 24 Oct email
([[2026-10-25_draft-reply-to-heather-finance]]). Is it true in principle? Not legal advice.

## Findings

- **D81 is not an alternative to mediation.** A D81 must go in with *every* consent order application,
  however the agreement was reached: directly, through solicitors, or through mediation. Mediation
  normally ends in a memorandum of understanding that a solicitor turns into a consent order plus D81.
- **Form E is mandatory only in contested court proceedings** (after a Form A application). It is also
  widely used *voluntarily* for disclosure in mediation and solicitor-led negotiation, including by
  couples who agree. Mediators require full disclosure, often on Form E or a similar form.
- **Mediation is for couples who need help agreeing**, not for those who "can't agree". Couples who
  truly can't agree go to court (Form A, then Form E). Alan himself recommended early mediation (2 Jun).
- **What is true:** Form E-level disclosure plus court is the route when agreement fails; a D81 with a
  consent order is what agreed couples file; and Alan advised (3 Aug) that a D81 was enough here.
- **Risk:** full and frank disclosure is still required on the consent-order route; an order can be set
  aside for non-disclosure. Heather's solicitor could easily rebut the line as written.

## Verdict

Half true. The Form E half is roughly right (it's the court/contested standard); the mediation half
and the "either/or" framing are wrong. Suggested replacement given to Rupert in console.
