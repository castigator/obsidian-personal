# Record of Terms Agreed: what it should contain (5 Nov 2026)

*Not legal advice. A working checklist to agree with Heather, then send to Alan to turn into the consent order.*

## Question

On the phone on 5 Nov 2026 Alan said Rupert can share the D81 with Heather, and pressed hard for an agreed Record of Terms. Rupert asked what we know about it, and what the document should include.

## What we know

- **Alan, 3 Aug 2026 email:** he needs (1) a Record of Terms Agreed between Rupert and Heather, **signed and dated by both**, which he drafts the financial (consent) order from, and (2) each party's draft D81 figures. See [[2026-08-03_alan-d81-vs-form-e-answer]].
- **Rupert's 1 Nov email, Q11:** asked what format he wants. No written answer yet. The 5 Nov call restated the request but, as far as we know, didn't specify a format.
- **What it is:** a plain-English summary of the deal, not a court document. The D81 *describes* an agreed order. It doesn't *create* one. The Record of Terms is the agreement itself. A solicitor then turns it into the formal wording of the consent order.
- **Why it matters:** signed terms reached with disclosure can carry real weight, and a court will usually hold parties to them unless there's a good reason not to (Xydhias and Edgar line of cases). So it's more than a note to Alan. Worth getting right before signing.

## Proposed contents

### 1. Header
- Names, case number 1780-7415-8911-6303, date.
- Statement that these are the terms agreed in full settlement of all financial claims, intended to be put into a consent order, subject to the court's approval.
- Each party has given full disclosure (D81 figures exchanged, with date) and has had the chance to take independent legal advice.

### 2. The house (Maple Tree House)
- **What happens:** repaired under the subsidence claim (6843593K), then sold. Option for a cash settlement in place of the insurer's repairs, if both agree.
- **Trigger and deadline:** marketed once repairs are signed off. A longstop date (e.g. sell as-is if repairs haven't started by X).
- **Conduct of sale:** who chooses the agent, how the asking price is set (agent's advice / both agree), who can accept an offer.
- **Heather's buyout option:** first refusal at market value, if she wants it.
- **Occupation until sale:** Heather lives there. Her responsibilities (keep it in good order, allow viewings and access for repairs).

### 3. Sale proceeds: order of deductions
- Mortgage (RBS 11177343), estate agent and conveyancing costs, the subsidence excess (£1,000), joint debts outstanding at completion.
- **Split the balance by percentage (50/50), not fixed sums.** The price, mortgage and debts will all have moved by then.

### 4. Costs until the sale
- Mortgage interest, buildings insurance, council tax, home emergency cover, utilities, repairs and maintenance: who pays what (currently 80% Rupert / 20% Heather).
- Whether that continues unchanged until completion, or changes at a set date.
- Whether any of it is credited back from the proceeds (or expressly not).
- That these are interim contributions, not maintenance.

### 5. Joint debts
- List them: Barclaycard 5002, Halifax 3123 (and the Virgin card it partly moved to), Ann's Barclaycard, loans from Ann (£3,318.00 + £5,522.66).
- Serviced 80/20 until the sale, then paid from the proceeds.
- No new borrowing on them.

### 6. Personal debts and other assets
- Each keeps their own bank accounts, savings, car and personal debts.
- House contents and chattels: how they're divided (and the cats).

### 7. Pensions
- Each keeps their own, no pension sharing order.
- Conditional on Heather's formal CETVs broadly matching her figures (~£195,508). Say what happens if they don't.
- Prudential trust: Heather (as Heather Jeffrey) to sign whatever is needed as co-trustee.

### 8. Maintenance and clean break
- No spousal maintenance either way. Clean break on completion of the sale (or on the order, with the interim payments written into it).
- Inheritance Act claims against each other's estates dismissed.
- Lucy: Rupert's £200/mo food money is voluntary and outside the order (or say if it isn't).

### 9. Insurance claims
- Subsidence: who deals with GHG, and any cash settlement goes to the house.
- Drain claim (6866220H): £400 excess split £200 each if ever billed.

### 10. Practical
- Each pays their own legal costs. Court fee (£58) split.
- Who drafts and files (Alan), and the Final Order isn't applied for until the consent order is sealed.
- What happens if either dies before the sale.
- Signed and dated by both.

## Gaps before it can be signed

- Heather's own figures on the D81 (capital, debts, income) and her formal pension statements.
- "Vaguely equal" vs 50/50: the split itself isn't agreed yet.
- Alan's answer to Q2 (how a deferred sale is written into the order) shapes sections 2–4.
- Format: ask Alan for a template or example. That avoids redrafting.

## Recommended actions

- Ask Alan for his preferred format or template (Q11).
- Use this list as the agenda with Heather. Agree the principles first, then the numbers.
- Draft the document once the split and the house mechanics are agreed.
