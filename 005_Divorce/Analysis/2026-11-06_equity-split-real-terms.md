# Equity split in real terms (contribution-adjusted) — 6 Nov 2026

*Working notes for Rupert to play with. Not legal advice. Private: not for sharing with Heather in this form.*

## Why

Heather has asked for **80% of the equity**. Rupert's view: on an 80/20 contribution basis that would hand her the best part of 90–95% of their life's savings in real terms, which isn't reasonable. He needs to reply to her soon. Before that, he wants to run some permutations: what actual split of the proceeds would give a target real-terms share (e.g. 80% or 75%).

## The basic sum (£100 pot, A puts in £80, B puts in £20)

| Split taken out (A / B) | A gets back per £1 put in | B gets back per £1 put in | Real-terms share (A / B) |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| 50 / 50 | 0.625 | 2.50 | 20 / 80 |
| 20 / 80 | 0.25 | 4.00 | 5.9 / 94.1 |

"Real-terms share" means each person's return per £1 put in, as a share of both returns added together. It's what the split would be if both had put in the same amount.

## Working backwards: what split gives a target real-terms share?

With contributions cA and cB, and A's target real-terms share s, A's share of the proceeds is:

**x = s × cA ÷ (s × cA + (1 − s) × cB)**

At 80/20 contributions (Rupert = A):

| Heather's real-terms share | Rupert's share of proceeds | Heather's share of proceeds |
|---:|---:|---:|
| 80% | 50.0% | 50.0% |
| 75% | 57.1% | 42.9% |
| 70% | 63.6% | 36.4% |
| 60% | 72.7% | 27.3% |
| 50% | 80.0% | 20.0% |

So on an 80/20 contribution basis, a straight 50/50 split already gives Heather 80% in real terms.

## Inputs to settle before using this

- **Which contribution ratio, over what period?** 80/20 is the current interim arrangement for Pebmarsh costs. The ratio over the whole marriage (mortgage, deposit, savings) may be different. The result depends heavily on this.
- **Which pot:** house equity only, or all assets including pensions?
- **Pot size:** e.g. house net proceeds £207,548.65 (D81 Q10 basis: £550k sale) or as-is equity.
