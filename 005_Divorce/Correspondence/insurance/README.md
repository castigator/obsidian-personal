### Drain Claim — Policy Wording Analysis

The drain damage claim falls under **Section A: Buildings Insurance, item 12: "Underground pipes, drains and cables"** (Policy Booklet p.23):

> "We'll pay claims for the cost of repairing the fabric of cables, underground pipes, drains and tanks serving your home if they're accidentally broken."

**Section B (Accidental Damage)** explicitly redirects drainage claims (p.27):
> "Claims relating to subsidence or drainage must be made under the buildings section of this policy."

**Excess:** Standard buildings excess of **£400** (£100 compulsory + £300 voluntary) applies — NOT the £1,000 subsidence excess, because this is a Section A buildings claim, not a subsidence claim.

**"Blockage of sewer pipes: No cover"** in the policy summary refers to clearing blockages only. The drain claim is for **structural repair** of accidentally broken drains (root ingress), which IS covered under item 12.

**Exclusion check (item 12):**
- "Anyone other than Gold policyholders when clearing blocked drains, unless the blockage is caused by structural damage to the drain itself" — the blockages ARE caused by structural damage (root ingress), so this exclusion does not apply.
- "Damage to drains serving your home which are not your responsibility" — these are on the property, so fine.

**Expected recovery: ~£8,960** (£9,360.63 repair cost − £400 excess) — ⚠️ **this net-recovery figure
has never actually been confirmed by Ageas/Catalyst**; it's Rupert's own modelling from the DW
Solutions quote. Catalyst's own completion report (12 Aug 2026 — see
[2026-08-12_catalyst-drainage-report-scope-mismatch.md](2026-08-12_catalyst-drainage-report-scope-mismatch.md))
describes a smaller, differently-referenced scope than the original DW Solutions survey, with no
cost breakdown at all — worth chasing before treating this figure as settled.
