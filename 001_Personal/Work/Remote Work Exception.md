## Section 2 — Business justification

### 1. Role and operational necessity

Rupert is a Technical Architect on the Fusion Aggregator platform, covering the RGP and Fusion platforms and vendor and operator integrations. He worked fully remotely, with Remote status formally approved, from April 2023 until 30 September 2026. In that time he led the architecture for a number of key business initiatives, and introduced new ways of working that the wider Technical Architecture team has adopted.

Most of Rupert's day-to-day work is with teams outside the UK. Currently that is predominantly the Fusion engineering teams in Sofia, Bulgaria. He also works closely with Trident in India on third-party integrations, with the infrastructure teams in India, and with teams in Israel. All of this collaboration happens on Teams, so working from Shell Mex House would not bring him any closer to the people he works with most. I and some of the other Technical Architects are based in London. Rupert keeps in regular contact with us over Teams, and that has always worked well. His work is architecture design, specification writing and technical review, and none of it depends on being in an office.

### 2. Onsite alternatives

Rupert lives in Suffolk, and there is no Aristocrat office nearer than Shell Mex House. Each office day means a 4 hour 20 minute round trip by car, train and Underground. On the standard three-day hybrid pattern that is 13 hours a week, about 76 working days a year, spent travelling, at a cost of roughly £9,000 a year for the season ticket, station parking and mileage. Fewer office days would cut the time but not remove it, and moving to a nearer hub isn't possible. Rupert will attend critical meetings in person when absolutely necessary, with reasonable notice. That way we still get face-to-face time where it matters, without the recurring cost of regular commuting.

### 3. Business impact and risk

Rupert has more than ten years' continuous service and holds deep knowledge of the RGP and Fusion platforms and of vendor and operator integrations. If this request is not approved, about 1.6 working days a week of his time would go on travel rather than delivery. Office days would also cut into his overlap with the teams in India, Sofia and Israel, and make him less available to help with live issues outside core hours. The commute would put real financial and personal pressure on him, which is a clear retention risk. Replacing his knowledge and experience would be expensive and slow, and would put current priorities at risk.

### 4. Team and collaboration impact

Because most of Rupert's working relationships are with teams in Bulgaria, India and Israel, having him in the London office would make little difference to collaboration or knowledge sharing. His regular contact with me and the London-based Technical Architects already works well over Teams, and his remote status has never caused a problem in the team. Remote working also gives more overlap with the overseas teams. India is 5½ hours ahead of the UK, so much of the shared working day falls in the UK morning, exactly when an office day would have Rupert on a train. Sofia and Israel are two hours ahead, so their afternoon finishes early in the UK day. His remote arrangement has worked for three and a half years without any negative effect on collaboration. He has continued to support and coach various teams, and restoring the arrangement keeps a pattern that has already been shown to work.

### 5. Accountability and onsite attendance

Rupert's performance is regularly monitored through his agreed objectives and the Connect review process, and is measured on deliverables. His work and productivity have always been exemplary, including throughout his three and a half years of remote working. He will keep core-hours availability on Teams. He is also regularly available outside core hours to help with live issues, which working remotely makes much easier. He will attend critical meetings in person when absolutely necessary, with reasonable notice, and travels for business when needed.

### 6. Duration and reassessment

This is an ongoing arrangement. Rupert's remote working has a three-and-a-half-year track record, so I don't see a need for a scheduled review. It would be reassessed if it stopped working for the business, for example through a sustained drop in performance or availability, or if Rupert's circumstances changed, such as a move to within practical commuting distance of Shell Mex House. His performance will continue to be monitored through the normal Connect process.

## Section 3 — Recommendation and review

**Manager recommendation:** Restore Rupert's Remote status, which he held from April 2023 to September 2026. Over that time he has shown he can deliver consistently to a high standard, working with teams that are mostly outside the UK. Requiring regular office attendance would cost about 1.6 working days a week in travel and create a clear retention risk, with no matching business benefit.
