# Plan: financing a ~£1,982 solicitor payment at 0% interest

Context (2026-07-21): solicitor invoice is **£1,982**, payable by card. Plan
is to route it through NatWest and M&S so the whole amount sits on 0%
interest, rather than paying standard purchase rates (24.9%–39.94%
depending on card).

**Status: EXECUTED 2026-07-28.** Figures below are what actually happened —
some deviated from the original plan (Atkins Dellow's phone system capped
the card payment at £1,900, not the full £1,982).

## What actually happened

1. **Paid Atkins Dellow via phone** — their card system only allowed £1,900
   on the NatWest Mastercard, not the full £1,982. **Rupert decided not to
   bank-transfer the remaining £82 separately** — it's being left as a
   standard-rate balance on NatWest rather than settled directly.
2. **Balance-transferred £1,900 from NatWest to M&S** (M&S's own stated
   limit on the day, below the theoretical £1,932.55 max) — 0% for 12
   months, fee 3.49%.
3. Since the £82 wasn't paid separately, it **does sit stranded on
   NatWest at standard rate** — same shape as the original plan's ~£49.45
   stranded remainder, just a bigger amount. Not a real problem: NatWest's
   existing £300/month direct debit already exceeds the minimum, and UK
   payment-allocation rules (Consumer Credit Act) require any surplus above
   the minimum to clear the highest-interest balance first — so the £82
   should clear itself within the first payment cycle or two, at the cost
   of roughly £1–2 in interest. Decided 28 Jul: not worth a separate
   transfer for that.
4. **M&S Direct Debit Amend Form signed 28/07/2026**, changed from Minimum
   to **Fixed £175.00/month** (card ending 0645, statement date 13/03, due
   date 01/08).

Why not transfer to NatWest instead of via it: NatWest's 0% offer was a
one-time intro deal from account opening (16 Feb 2026) — no evidence of a
standing repeat offer, so new transfers in now would likely hit standard
26.436% APR, not 0%. Why not charge M&S directly: that would sit at M&S's
*purchase* rate (24.9%), since the 0% offer only applies to balances
transferred in, not to spending on the card itself.
